Start with the practical picture.

A typical Florida car crash often becomes a documentation and timing problem before it becomes a legal problem. The other driver's insurer may reach out quickly. Your own records may still be incomplete. This page focuses on what is usually useful to organize early.

What usually happens in the first days

  • Police or crash reports, medical visits, photos, and insurance contact often arrive in a short window.
  • People frequently underestimate how fast the other insurer may ask for a recorded statement or medical authorization.
  • Having a basic timeline and file of records reduces pressure when those requests arrive.

Information that tends to matter later

  • Vehicle positions, damage photos, license plates, weather and roadway conditions.
  • Names and contact details for any witnesses who stopped.
  • Names of responding agencies and any report or case numbers provided at the scene.
  • A short written account of what you remember while details are still clear.

Insurance conversations

  • You can be polite and still avoid guessing about fault, speed, or injury severity when you do not know.
  • Ask for the claim number and the adjuster's contact information in writing when possible.
  • Keep copies of anything you send or sign.

PRESERVE EARLY

Items worth keeping organized

  1. Scene and vehicle photographs from multiple angles
  2. Witness names and phone numbers
  3. Crash report or agency contact information
  4. Medical visit notes and discharge papers
  5. Towing, storage, and repair estimates
  6. Any written communications with insurers
